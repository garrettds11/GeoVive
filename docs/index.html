GeoVivé is a map for exploring places and keeping your own collections of pinned locations. Anyone can browse public maps; sign in to create and edit your own.

Explore the map

  • Move around: drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Inspect a place: click a point on the map to see its name, type and notes.
  • Fly to a place: open Features in the sidebar and click an item. The map zooms in to street level.
  • Change the look: use Map Style for Streets, Outdoors, Light, Dark or Satellite.
  • Filter by type: each dataset has its own categories, shown with their colors and counts in the legend. Tick or untick a category to show or hide it.
  • Measure distance: the scale bars in the bottom-left corner show miles/feet and kilometers/meters.
  • Status: the upper-right corner shows the map connection, current zoom level and version. On a phone they move to the bottom-right.

On a phone, the sidebar is a tray: tap the bar with three lines to open or close it.

Datasets

A dataset is a collection of places. Pick one from Dataset in the sidebar. GeoVivé opens with World Capitals, the national capitals of the world from Natural Earth. When you're signed in, your own maps appear in the list too.

Layers

Open Layers in the sidebar to stack more information on the map. Your choices are remembered in this browser.

  • More datasets: tick any other dataset to show its points alongside the one you're browsing. Click a point to see which dataset it belongs to.
  • Overlays: live maps from public agencies, drawn directly from their servers: USGS topo, shaded relief and imagery, streams and lakes, BLM land ownership, National Forest boundaries, and state and county lines. Each has a Source link and an opacity slider.

Overlays cover the United States and depend on the agency's servers; if one is slow or down, that layer stays blank while the rest of the map works. Land ownership is detailed down to about zoom 14.

Signing in

Browsing needs no account. To create maps, click Sign in, then either:

  • Sign in with Google: your GeoVivé account is created the first time. Google shows an account chooser each time, so you can switch accounts.
  • Email and password: choose Create an account, enter your email and a password, then type the verification code we email you. Forgot your password? Use the reset link on the sign-in page.

One email address is one GeoVivé account. If you created an account with email and password and later choose Sign in with Google using the same verified address, both methods open the same account and maps. (The very first time, you may need to click Sign in with Google a second time.) If you first signed in with Google, keep using Google for that address.

  • Sign out signs you out of GeoVivé only. It does not sign you out of Google itself; on a shared computer, sign out of Google too.
  • You stay signed in for about a day before GeoVivé asks you to sign in again.

My maps

After signing in, the My maps panel lists maps you own, with their pin counts.

  1. Type a name in New map name.
  2. Tick Public if anyone should be able to view it (maps are private by default).
  3. Click Create. The new, empty map opens, ready for pins.

Click any map in the list to open it. To remove a map and all its pins, open it and click Delete map. This can't be undone.

Adding and editing pins

Open one of your maps to show the edit bar.

  1. Click + Add pin. The cursor becomes a crosshair.
  2. Click the spot on the map.
  3. Enter a Name, choose a Type (Location, Event or Alert) and add any Notes.
  4. Click Save.

To change or remove a pin, click it on the map, edit the form, then Save or Delete. Press Esc to cancel adding a pin.

Public and private maps

  • Private maps are visible only to you, even to someone who has the link.
  • Public maps can be viewed by anyone, signed in or not. Only you can edit them.

FAQ

How do I switch Google accounts?

Sign out, then click Sign in and pick a different account from Google's chooser, or choose Use another account.

I can't see the edit buttons.

Editing is only available on maps you own, while signed in. Public maps from others are view-only.

Where does GeoVivé's data come from?

Public datasets list their source (for example, World Capitals comes from Natural Earth, which is public domain). Your maps contain only what you add.
