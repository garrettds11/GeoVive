The world is big.
Make it yours.

GeoVivé is a map for exploring places and keeping your own collections of them: the trailhead, the hidden campsite, the café worth the detour. Take the two-minute tour, or jump to the answers you need.

Get started
Step 1 · Start in a minute

Three steps from blank map to your map

No setup, no install. GeoVivé runs in your browser on a computer, tablet or phone.

  • Look around. Drag, zoom and click any point. No account needed.
  • Sign in. With Google or an email address. It's free.
  • Make a map. Name it, then drop pins wherever life takes you.
Step 2 · Explore the map

Move freely, then look closer

Everything starts on the map. On a phone, the sidebar is a tray you open with the bar at the top.

  • Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Click any point for its name, type and notes.
  • Features lists every place; click one and the map flies there. Tick categories in the legend to filter.
  • Top-left tools: find yourself, reset north with the compass, lock north-up, or tilt into 3D terrain.
  • Map Style switches between Streets, Outdoors, Light, Dark and Satellite.
Step 3 · Layers

Stack what matters on one map

Open Layers to lay more information over the map. Your choices are remembered on this device.

For you

Show other datasets beside your own, and turn on live public maps: USGS topo, relief and imagery, streams and lakes, BLM land ownership, National Forests, state and county lines. See where public land meets the trail you're planning.

From the apps you use

Apps connected to GeoVivé can bring their own layers, like hunting units or service areas. Arrive from one and its layers appear under AppConnect, alongside your own maps, for that visit.

Public overlays come straight from each agency and cover the United States. Shapes are simplified for display, so check the official source for exact boundaries.

Step 4 · Your maps

Sign in, then make it personal

Browsing never needs an account. Sign in with Google or an email and password to start keeping your own maps.

  • Private by default. Only you see a new map, even if someone has the link. Tick Public to share it for viewing; only you can edit.
  • Drop pins with Add pin or Pin my location, or Save to my map from any pin you find. Your location stays on your device unless you save a pin.
  • Make pins yours: Markdown notes, emoji (the smiley button, or Win+. / Ctrl+Cmd+Space), and copy any pin as Markdown or JSON.
Step 5 · Import & export

Bring data in. Take it anywhere.

Your maps aren't locked in. Open one of your maps to move places in and out.

Import data

A GeoJSON file (up to 50 MB), a GeoJSON link, or an ArcGIS layer from an agency, with an optional filter. Choose which fields become names and categories. Up to 20,000 places, with their source remembered.

Export

GeoJSON for GIS and map apps, KML for Google Earth, GPX for GPS units and outdoor apps. Pins become waypoints, lines become tracks, at full detail.

That's the tour. Answers to everything else are just below.

Frequently Asked Questions

The details behind the tour. Tap a question to open it.

Getting around

How do I find a place quickly?

Open Features in the sidebar and click an item; the map flies to it. Filter by category with the legend, and switch datasets under Dataset. GeoVivé opens with World Capitals from Natural Earth.

How do I find public maps?

Type in Search public maps at the top of the Dataset panel. It looks through every public map's name, description and tags as you type; tap a #tag to narrow the results, and use the arrow keys and Enter on a keyboard. Opening a map adds it to the Dataset menu under Public maps, next to Your maps.

What do the buttons in the corners do?

Top-left: find your location, the compass (turns with the map; click to reset north), the lock (keeps north up) and 3D terrain. Bottom-left: scale bars in miles and kilometers. Top-right (bottom-right on a phone): the map connection and zoom level.

Is my location shared?

No. The location button and Pin my location use your position only in your browser, after it asks you. It's saved only if you save a pin there.

Why is an overlay blank?

Public overlays load straight from each agency and cover the United States. If an agency's server is slow or down, that layer stays blank while the rest of the map works. Land ownership has detail down to about zoom 14.

Where do the layers under AppConnect come from?

From an app or business connected to GeoVivé, shown when you arrive from that app or open a map it created, for that visit. They belong to the provider and show its credits. Shapes are simplified for display; check the provider's official sources for exact boundaries.

Accounts & your maps

How do I sign in?

Click Sign in, then either Sign in with Google (your account is created the first time) or Create an account with email and password and type the code we email you. Forgot your password? Use the reset link on the sign-in page. Sessions last about a day.

I used email before; can I switch to Google?

Yes. One email address is one GeoVivé account: choosing Google with the same verified address opens the same maps. The very first time you may need to click Sign in with Google twice. If you started with Google, keep using Google.

How do I switch Google accounts or sign out?

Sign out, click Sign in and pick another account from Google's chooser. Signing out of GeoVivé doesn't sign you out of Google, so on a shared computer sign out of Google too.

Who can see my maps?
  • Private (the default): only you, even with the link.
  • Public: anyone can view, signed in or not; only you can edit.
How do I create, open or delete a map?
  1. Sign in and open My maps.
  2. Type a name, tick Public if you want to share it, and click Create.
  3. Click any map in the list to open it. Delete map removes the map and all its pins, and can't be undone.
How do I add, edit or delete pins?

Open one of your maps, click Add pin, then click the spot (or use Pin my location). Give it a name, type and notes, and Save. To change a pin, click it, then the pencil. Esc cancels adding a pin.

Can I save a pin from someone else's map?

Yes. Click any pin on a map you don't own (a public map, or another dataset you've turned on under Layers) and choose Save to my map. Pick one of your maps, or name a new one, and a copy is added there with a note of where it came from. The copy is yours to edit; the original doesn't change.

What can I put in pin notes?

Simple Markdown: **bold**, *italic*, - lists, headings and links. Emoji from the smiley button or your keyboard (Win+. on Windows, Ctrl+Cmd+Space on a Mac). Codes like U+1F3E0 show as the character. Each pin card copies the pin as Markdown or JSON.

How do I help people find my public map?

Open your map and click Details in the edit bar. Give it a clear name, a description of what's on it and who it's for, and a few tags (like hiking, colorado, trailheads), then tick Public. Search matches all three.

How do I review everything I've pinned, or check what's public?

Open My data (also linked from My maps on the map). It lists every pin across your maps, and warns you if any are in a public map. You can:

  • Search and filter by map, type, or public vs private.
  • Switch a whole map between public and private.
  • Create pins by coordinates, edit names, types, notes and positions, and delete pins.
  • Tick several pins to delete them, or move or copy them to another map, for example out of a public map into a private one.
I can't see the edit buttons.

Editing appears only on maps you own, while you're signed in. Other people's public maps are view-only.

Importing & exporting

What can I import?
  • GeoJSON file: .geojson or .json, up to 50 MB.
  • GeoJSON link: a public web address, such as an open-data download.
  • ArcGIS layer: an address ending in FeatureServer/0 or MapServer/2, with an optional filter like STATE = 'CO'.

Under Options, pick the name and category fields and whether to add or replace. Imports run in the background, up to 20,000 features, and GeoVivé records where the data came from.

Which export format should I pick?
  • GeoJSON: GIS software and map apps; includes where imported data came from.
  • KML: Google Earth and most mapping apps.
  • GPX: GPS units and outdoor apps. Pins become waypoints; lines and area edges become tracks.

You can export public maps and your own maps, at full detail.

Trust & help

Where does GeoVivé's data come from?

Public datasets name their source; World Capitals comes from Natural Earth (public domain). AppConnect layers come from the connected app and show its credits. Your maps contain only what you add.

Can my business bring its own layers?

Yes, with AppConnect. You keep your data; GeoVivé shows it to your users. Register on the AppConnect page.

How do I report a problem?

Email admin@geovive.link. See Trust & Security for how we protect your data.
